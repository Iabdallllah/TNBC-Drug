3. Selectivity null and the “Predicted selective (unvalidated)” relabel

The restricted check — drugs with measured AUC < 0.5 in either line — does not rescue the correlation (n=66, Spearman 0.0287). The learned-227 subset is also null (Spearman 0.0460). The predicted Δ range is -0.0557 to 0.0385, while the MDA-MB-231 80% interval is ±0.093: the entire selectivity range is inside the noise band.

Appendix B. Short defence Q&A

Q1. “Your CatBoost model loses to a column mean. Why is it in the project?”
It is reported as a negative result. The baseline wins because CatBoost never learned 85% of drug identities. Within the 227 drugs it did learn, it ties the baseline. The expression contribution is unproven, and we say so. The project’s value is the audit that found this, not the model.

Q2. “Did the sealed prediction or the ‘selective’ labels rely on this model?”
The panel was chosen on mechanism (GPX4, system xc⁻, iron) and agreed with the lab before any plate. The model outputs are a frozen, falsifiable record, not the justification. Predicted selectivity is uncorrelated with measured selectivity, so the four “selective” drugs are relabelled unvalidated hypotheses. Selectivity will be tested by SI = IC50(MCF7)/IC50(MDA-MB-231) on the plate. We will report Spearman with a bootstrap CI after unblinding, as the PRD requires.

Q3. “If the model is this weak, what is the scientific contribution?”
A reproducible pipeline whose own controls caught its failures: a circular-label inflation of 0.65–0.99, a substring bug that would have mislabelled Solifenacin as NAC, an 84.7% plateau, and a dead selectivity threshold. We found them, bounded them and tested the fixes. We also pre-registered a falsifiable wet-lab panel, with CTRPv2 showing MDA-MB-231 in the most RSL3-sensitive 30% while MDA-MB-468 is resistant.
